# Associative assembly mates

Assembly geometry contract version 4 stores mate intent independently of the kernel, solver, renderer and widgets. Primary references identify direct component occurrences by stable ID and then an optional sequence of nested occurrence IDs. Auxiliary references retain the same complete identity. A source can be an origin plane/axis, exact planar face, proven rotational face axis, straight/circular edge axis, datum, coordinate-system element or complete Z/X frame, solved sketch point/axis, expression-defined local point, exact planar sketch/model curve, or closed planar profile/face centroid. Provenance and current geometry ownership remain separate. A complete coordinate frame supplies its local Z axis and local X transverse direction, retaining twist orientation for rotation locking and mechanical phase relationships.

A mate's optional boxed `relationship` contains typed advanced/mechanical intent. Existing source schema 1 files keep their numeric frames and standard mate fields. All added optional fields omit `None`; absent relationships retain the existing behavior and pinned-source hashes. Associative references never fall back to numeric frames when missing, suppressed, ambiguous or unresolved. Evaluated frames, auxiliary frames, motion diagnostics and curve carriers are application-owned f64 data. The current integrated solver fingerprint is `tcad-native-lm/14`; adapter composition records its own fingerprint.

The docked editor groups the types into Standard, Advanced and Mechanical. Geometry selection, expressions, placement and parameter changes enter the common document draft and persistent worker. OK requires the current draft's validated evaluation and commits one undoable operation; Cancel restores its checkpoint. Invalid or unsynchronized Advanced Source blocks Apply. Captured mechanical motion zeros and rigid lock poses use the current validated placements. Geometry-dependent commands and state are also available through the semantic presentation contract.

## Standard modes

- Coincident covers plane/plane coincidence and orientation, point/plane incidence, axis/plane incidence, point/axis incidence, axis/axis coincidence, and point/point coincidence.
- Planar aligns plane normals and enforces a signed normal gap.
- Parallel and Perpendicular handle plane/plane, axis/axis and plane/axis references with the proper interpretation of a plane normal.
- Concentric aligns exact axes, including supported cylinder/cone faces and circular edge/sketch axes. Alignment can be either, aligned or opposed. An optional angle expression locks relative rotation.
- Distance uses signed plane separation, nonnegative point separation, and nonnegative radial point/axis or parallel-axis separation.
- Angle uses 0–180° directional angles and 0–90° plane/axis angles. Opposed alignment reverses the second direction.
- Lock captures and retains all six relative placement coordinates.

## Advanced modes

**Width** uses two distinct parallel width walls on the first component and two distinct parallel tab walls on the second. Centered, free travel, distance from the lower projected width wall, and fractional available travel modes are supported. All modes enforce containment and reject a tab wider than the available width. Fraction is dimensionless and lies in 0..1. Free travel uses inequality bounds and retains the sliding degree of freedom.

**Symmetric** reflects the two selected origins/points about an associative planar reference. Directional frames also receive the reflected direction with the selected alignment. The symmetry plane can belong to another component. This is frame/point symmetry; it does not assert that arbitrary component solids are mirror images.

**Limit Distance** bounds signed planar separation or point/radial-axis separation. Two plane normals remain aligned. **Limit Angle** bounds directional angles in 0–180°. Bounds are unit-aware expressions. A bound constrains travel without converting it into a fixed distance/angle.

**Profile Center** mates the area centroids of two closed planar sketches or planar model faces, aligns their normals, and optionally locks relative in-plane rotation. Inner boundaries subtract holes. A sketch must produce one material region; disconnected regions and open profiles need an explicit feature/face selection. Centroids use Green boundary integrals over exact positive-weight rational carriers with adaptive f64 quadrature, rather than tessellated polygons. Rational degree is limited to 32 and weight ratio to 10000; the estimated centroid error must be at most 1e-7 mm and the computation has a bounded subdivision/evaluation budget. Nonplanar/implicit carriers, degenerate area, ill-conditioned integrals and failed estimates are reported explicitly. The numerical error estimate is not a certified exact-arithmetic bound.

**Linear Coupler** relates the axial displacement coordinates of two captured reference axes in an explicit support occurrence or the assembly universe. The positive dimensionless ratio, phase/length offset, and direction are editable. Axis directions stay fixed relative to that support; separate locating mates establish the guides.

**Path** places a follower point/axis origin on an exact planar curve. Free position adds a solved carrier parameter; a fixed expression specifies its normalized parameter in 0..1. Optional orientation aligns the follower axis with the tangent or the path plane normal. Sketch lines, circular arcs/circles, ellipses, Beziers and positive rational splines use current solved sketch geometry and its associative plane. Planar model edges retain their exact explicit positive-weight rational carrier and its parameterization. Open paths bound the parameter; closed carriers wrap it. Fraction denotes carrier parameter, not a claim of arc-length percentage.

## Mechanical modes

**Hinge** combines two collinear axes and an axial seating plane on each component. The planes must be normal to the axes; their seating gap is zero. Optional signed rotation limits lie within −180..180° in the selected local frames. The supported hinge retains its spin degree of freedom inside those bounds.

**Gear** relates captured shaft angles using a positive dimensionless ratio and angular phase. Default direction is opposing rotation; Reverse changes it. **Rack and Pinion** relates the first shaft's rotation to the second axis's axial travel, with positive travel per revolution and a length phase. **Screw** relates coaxial shafts' relative rotation to their relative axial travel by positive lead per revolution and length phase; opposed shaft directions are handled explicitly. Both shafts/nut can rotate or translate. Separate seating/guide mates determine any other movement.

**Universal Joint** relates intersecting shaft rotations with the captured shaft-axis angle through the angular transmission relation. Relative center coincidence is enforced. Perpendicular captured shafts are singular and rejected. Phase and transmission direction are editable. This is a kinematic shaft relationship, not generated yoke hardware or dynamic simulation.

These couplers measure motion in the selected support frame and keep their captured axis directions in that frame. Captured f64 zero frames are source intent, not stale worker geometry. Angular coordinates use the selected principal ±180° branch; persistent revolution counters, continuous multi-turn motion driving and alternate winding selection remain implementation work. A support occurrence must differ from the two moving participants. When no instance is explicitly fixed, a selected mechanical support is grounded before falling back to the first occurrence.

**Slot** uses a line or circular center path and a point/pin-axis origin, with the same free/fixed parameter and optional orientation controls as Path. Exact model carriers are eligible when the line/circle identity proof succeeds. Their rational parameterization is retained; a retracing rational carrier is never silently replaced by an endpoint segment or simplified arc. This mode describes the pin center's path; it does not infer a center path from arbitrary slot-wall faces.

**Cam / Roller** places a point follower or roller center on the selected planar profile's normal offset by a nonnegative roller radius. Radius zero is a point follower. The selected side follows the directed curve; an axis follower is normal to the profile plane. The contact parameter can be free or expression-driven. Analytic and rational planar sketch/model carriers are supported, with exact carrier derivatives. Singular tangents fail explicitly. This is local profile contact: it does not claim arbitrary 3D surface tangency, global collision exclusion, concave-profile self-contact resolution, or physical force simulation.

## Mate-aware Move Component and Rotate Component

Assembly manipulation contract version 1 carries a transient f64 target independently of document definitions, solver objects, and rendering. Move Component, Rotate Component, and combined Move + Rotate are available from component controls and command search. The docked editor offers live numeric drag dials and unit-aware/named-parameter expressions for assembly X/Y/Z offsets and rotations, with a component-origin or picked-point pivot. Each field, mode, pivot, status, and confirmation action has a stable semantic identity and ordinary user style properties.

The selected occurrence retains its complete root-relative path. A nested clicked face or point drives its direct top-level rigid subassembly as one unit; it never silently edits a shared nested definition. Suppressed components, pattern-generated copies, explicit fixed components, the solver's automatic grounded reference, expression-driven positions, and fully constrained components receive explicit movement reasons. Pattern seeds remain eligible and their copies follow the resulting placements. Manipulation currently operates in the document root assembly; edit an assembly as root to move its internal participants. It does not implement flexible subassembly scopes.

A separate persistent worker solves the current requested pose against the current exact local mate and auxiliary frames, including mechanical coupling and travel bounds. The requested pure move/rotation seeds the solve; engineering equations dominate the transient objective, and a final strict mate projection must converge before a result can enter the shared draft. Unreachable target position/rotation differences are exposed in the editor. The source placement expressions also participate as hard translation equations, rather than movable placement guesses. Free Path/Slot/Cam parameter seeds follow the solved pose so the normal geometry rebuild starts on the same carrier position. Persisted seeds use the same 0..1 carrier-domain mapping as mate equations: modulo 1 for closed curves and bounded contact for open curves, preserving seam/end contact while satisfying source validation. This is local numerical constrained manipulation; it does not establish global reachability, collision exclusion, or continuous multi-turn winding.

Both document identity and goal generation reject stale replies. Completed manipulation results remain queued while Settings owns the document-write lock. Adoption verifies that the intended document entered the shared draft, then normal current-source geometry validation must preserve the solved component poses within 1e-5 mm translation and 1e-7 radians rotation before Apply. OK waits for the current movement and geometry, commits one undoable change, and Cancel restores the starting shared checkpoint. Neither action introduces persistent driving mates. Placement seeds and canonical free Path/Slot/Cam parameter seeds are written to the active named configuration, or to Base when Base is active. The scope control publishes that ownership explicitly; Base editing is disabled while a named configuration is active because its overrides could mask the result. Select the intended configuration before beginning the tool. Configuration inheritance and fixed/expression/pattern ownership remain intact.

## Model viewport dragging

Primary dragging a movable component in the model opens Move Component after the drag threshold, retaining ordinary click selection. During an active Move/Rotate operation, dragging the selected rigid component or its gizmo owns the pointer until release, including outside the viewport. View-plane translation, assembly X/Y/Z translation arrows, assembly X/Y/Z rotation rings, and a view-normal rotation ring are implemented. The chosen component origin or picked pivot remains mechanical geometry; complete nested occurrence paths identify the selected top-level rigid component. Edge-on rotation planes and nearly view-aligned translation axes are disabled with a rotate-view explanation rather than amplifying projection noise.

Version 1 mechanical-space drag helpers use f64 ray/plane intersections, ray/axis closest approach and signed angles. Camera and gizmo pixels stay in the desktop adapter. All handles publish semantic origins, directions, kinds, paths, selected/dragging states and commands; their color, width, stroke and handle size use ordinary style rules. Selecting a semantic handle arms its supported gesture. Mode/pivot switches rebase transient goals at the accepted actual pose, so changing controls does not return a translated component to its original pivot. A transient preferred placement retains the starting orientation for position-only gestures without adding rotational mate equations.

Each capture freezes a current validated starting pose and shared draft snapshot. Navigation, drawing, model selection and menus cannot own the same gesture. Escape restores only the gesture-start snapshot, retaining earlier movement edits; operation Cancel retains its original checkpoint behavior. Opening Settings cancels a capture before the preferences checkpoint. Presentation replacement and viewport resize end obsolete capture while retaining its current draft. Movement samples the latest pointer while the native movement worker is idle, keeps each request stable through its solve, and submits the exact release value. Source/request/value guards reject superseded replies, and every edit cancels an earlier queued OK.

For an assembly without root component patterns or in-context Reference Body dependencies, successful movement can display the unchanged native part meshes at its solved f64 placements immediately while full geometry validation runs. This cache is marked invalid/display-only, preserves the last accepted pose during a newer pending goal, and never enters Session or supplies authoritative mate zero/lock frames. Root patterns and in-context geometry wait for their normal native rebuild instead. Full current-source evaluation and pose adoption still gate Apply. Collision exclusion, continuous mechanical winding, flexible subassembly motion and simultaneous multi-component manipulation remain engineering work.

## Evaluation, diagnostics and source identity

The worker builds placement-independent geometry, resolves each configured leaf's exact references, solves nested rigid subassemblies before their parents, and then rebuilds in-context geometry from solved placements. Simultaneous configurations have separate definition/configuration evaluation identities. All primary and auxiliary references participate in dependency closure; a closure entering an in-context Reference Body is rejected because its placement would feed back into the geometry used to solve that placement. Configured suppression removes mates involving suppressed primary, auxiliary or support components. References to suppressed local geometry fail explicitly.

Component patterns solve seeds first, expand stable driven occurrences, and validate copied internal mates at their resulting fixed poses. Auxiliary occurrence identities are remapped with the copied group. Mechanical zero frames follow a copied internal support or the copied transform relative to an external support/universe. Making a component unique retargets only primary/auxiliary feature sources whose complete occurrence path reaches it, preserving shared siblings. Library namespace/update operations map support/occurrence/feature/member IDs and expressions, while preserving labels, paths and other literal text.

Evaluations publish aggregate convergence/residual, per-mate maximum residual and local equation rank/redundancy, inequality counts, and solved free carrier parameters. A projected equality-Jacobian nullspace describes each seed component's translational/rotational motion directions and geometric DOF. Coupled components may share DOF, so per-component counts must not be summed. Travel bounds are excluded from the equality rank; zero-width Limit Distance, Limit Angle, and Hinge ranges remain equality constraints. Their restrictions still participate in solve/validation. These diagnostics describe local infinitesimal freedom, not finite reachability, a minimal conflict set, collision freedom, or dynamics. Nonconvergent results identify the unresolved mate records and block Apply. Angular residuals use 100 mm/radian alongside millimeter positional residuals and the solver's 1e-6 maximum residual tolerance.

Remaining assembly engineering includes general curved/spherical tangency, composite and 3D paths, broader automatic mating and repair, continuous multi-turn coordinates, simultaneous multi-component manipulation, flexible subassembly scopes, assembly features, broader native interference/clearance validation and large-assembly sparse performance. Compilation/formatting/linting provide the available implementation checks; no code tests, probes or manual/UI smoke exercises were written or run. Interactive acceptance remains with the user.
